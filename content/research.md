## Measuring emissions from power plants with deep learning

Power plants are among the largest point sources of carbon dioxide, but direct measurements of their emissions are sparse. We explore deep learning methods that estimate CO₂ emissions from individual power plants using satellite observations, working toward a framework that generalizes across plants and regions. We build off of this work to explore NOx emissions.

+ Murayama, H., Wang, S., Sherman, L., Cohen, R., Hsiang, S. *Parameterizing deep learning for carbon dioxide emission quantification for power plants.* In preparation.
+ Walimbe, P., Murayama, H,, Cohen R., [*Detecting Changes in Power Plant NOx Emissions Using Deep Learning and TEMPO Satellite Imagery*](https://eartharxiv.org/repository/view/15747/) Pre-print.

## Reconstructing historical land cover in Africa

Our picture of how African landscapes have changed over the past century is limited by the lack of historical data. Using an archive of roughly one million historical aerial photographs, We use deep learning to build high-resolution, historical land cover maps across Africa.

+ Noda, E.^*, Murayama, H.^*, Schneider, S.A.^*, et al. *Developing high resolution, historical land cover maps in Africa using deep learning and an aerial photography archive.* In preparation.

## Machine learning with satellite imagery

I contributed to a global evaluation of how well machine learning with satellite imagery can measure ground conditions across a wide range of variables.

+ Proctor, J., Carleton, T., Chong, T., et al. [*A global evaluation of machine learning with satellite imagery for measuring ground conditions.*](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5568706) NBER working paper; in review.

## Earlier work

+ **Conservation and ecosystems in Costa Rica.** Starting at [NASA DEVELOP](https://develop.larc.nasa.gov/), we worked on remote sensing projects in southwestern Costa Rica, including habitat suitability for jaguar corridors, sites for coral reef rehabilitation, forest cover and connectivity in protected areas ([*Remote Sensing*, 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs16061088)), and the effect of riparian vegetation on coastal turbidity ([*Communications Sustainability*, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44458-025-00031-5)).
+ **Voting rights.** As a fellow in the [UW eScience Institute's Data Science for Social Good program](https://escience.washington.edu/using-data-science/data-science-for-social-good/), our team worked on methods for estimating demographics in racially polarized voting analyses ([*Sociological Methods & Research*, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1177/00491241231192383)).
+ **Digital health social justice.** At [Berkeley's D-Lab](https://dlab.berkeley.edu/), I helped develop and apply a guide for social justice in digital health ([*Frontiers in Digital Health*, 2022](https://doi.org/10.3389/fdgth.2022.807886)).
+ **Biochemistry.** At Wellesley College, I studied DNA G-quadruplex formation in the bacterium *Bdellovibrio bacteriovorus* ([*Biochemistry*, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.biochem.2c00443)).
